# Code of Conduct

BREACH is a game about breaking into systems in order to learn how to protect them. Its community has to be the opposite of a hostile network: a place where beginners can ask anything, and where experts share without looking down on anyone.

This code applies to every BREACH space: the repositories of the `breach-game` organization (issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews), the missions published in the community catalogue, and any event or channel that speaks for the project.

## What we expect

- Be welcoming to people of every background, level and language. Many players are discovering security; there is no stupid question.
- Give feedback on the work, not on the person. Explain, suggest, link to a source.
- Accept that the maintainers decide what fits the game, and that a "no" to a mission or a feature is not a "no" to you.
- Credit other people's work and ideas.
- Respect the game's framework: everything stays simulated, every target fictional, every attack paired with its defence.

## What we do not accept

- Insults, harassment, intimidation, discriminatory jokes or language, sexualised content, and personal attacks, in public or in private.
- Publishing someone's personal information without their explicit permission.
- Using the project to target real people, companies or systems: real domains, real addresses, real credentials, working exploits or payloads. This includes "jokes" and "tests".
- Content unsuitable for players aged 16 and over: violence, shocking or hateful material.
- Repeatedly ignoring a maintainer's request to stop.

## Reporting

If something happens that breaks this code, or makes you uncomfortable, tell the maintainers privately. Write to the contact address shown on the [organization profile](https://github.com/breach-game), or use GitHub's **Report content** option on the comment or issue concerned. Every report is read, and kept confidential.

A security vulnerability is not a conduct problem: report it through the repository's **Security > Report a vulnerability** form (see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md)).

## What happens next

The maintainers will look at the report, may ask for more context, and will act in proportion to what happened:

1. **A note.** A private reminder of this code, and a request to correct course.
2. **A warning.** With a clear consequence if it happens again.
3. **A temporary ban** from the project's spaces.
4. **A permanent ban.** For serious or repeated harm. A community mission that targets real systems or people is removed from the catalogue at once.

## Origin

This code is inspired by the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/), version 2.1, adapted to a security education game.
